Eye education

Understand your eyes before you decide.

Short, plain-language explanations of the conditions we treat most often, written by the doctors who treat them. Bring your questions to your visit.

Cataracts

Cataracts

The lens inside your eye is normally clear. A cataract is that lens turning cloudy, usually slowly, as a natural part of aging.

What you notice. Blurry or dim vision, glare and halos around lights at night, colors that look faded, and glasses prescriptions that keep changing.

What we do. When a cataract starts interfering with your daily life, surgery replaces the cloudy lens with a clear artificial one. The procedure takes about 15 minutes, and most people see better within a day or two. See cataract surgery for the lens options.

Glaucoma

Glaucoma

Glaucoma is damage to the optic nerve, the cable that carries images from your eye to your brain, most often caused by pressure inside the eye.

What you notice. Usually nothing at first. Side vision goes first and so gradually that most people never feel it, which is why glaucoma is found at routine exams, not by symptoms.

What we do. Measure eye pressure, photograph the optic nerve and test your visual field. Treatment lowers pressure with drops, laser or surgery, and the vision you have is protected.

Laser refractive surgery

Laser refractive surgery

Nearsightedness, farsightedness and astigmatism come from the shape of the cornea. A laser can reshape it so light focuses correctly without glasses.

LASIK creates a thin flap in the cornea, reshapes the tissue underneath and lays the flap back. Recovery is fast, often overnight.

PRK reshapes the surface of the cornea directly, without a flap. Healing takes longer, but it is the better choice for thinner corneas and some active lifestyles.

A candidacy exam measures your cornea and prescription and tells you which, if either, is right for you.

Dry eyes

Dry eyes

Your eyes are coated by a film of tears that keeps them comfortable and the vision clear. Dry eye is that film breaking down, from too few tears or tears that evaporate too fast.

What you notice. Grittiness, burning, redness, tired eyes after screen time, and sometimes watering, because the eye overreacts to the dryness.

What helps. Blink fully and often, look away from screens every 20 minutes, and use preservative-free artificial tears. If drops stop helping, prescription treatments and lid therapy address the cause rather than the symptom.

Floaters

Floaters

Floaters are small shadows that drift across your vision, cast by tiny clumps in the gel that fills the eye. Most are harmless and become less noticeable over time.

When to call us the same day. A sudden shower of new floaters, flashes of light, or a curtain or shadow across part of your vision can mean a retinal tear or detachment, which needs prompt treatment.
